\section{External oscillating electric field\label{sec:External-oscillating-electric-field}}

Oscillating orthogonal electric field can bias the electrospinning
process, particularly the radius of the electrospun fibers. Further,
it can be used as a control mechanism to achieve uniform distributions
of polymer filaments at the collector. In JETSPIN it is possible to
simulate external oscillating electric field. Denoted the electric
field

\begin{equation}
\vec{E}=\left(E_{x},\,E_{y},\,E_{z}\right),
\end{equation}
the directive \texttt{external potential type} (see Table \ref{tab:inputlist})
can be used to select the waveform of the oscillation. The user can
select three types of waveform: a rectangular wave oriented along
x-axis; a rectangular wave produced by a RC circuit; a rotating electric
field of a sinusoidal wave. For the static field (type 0) and the
two rectangular waves (types 1 and 2) the amplitude is $E_{x}=\Phi_{0}/h$,
with $\Phi_{0}$ given by \texttt{external potential}. In the first case the electric field is
given by

\begin{equation}
\vec{E}=\left(E_{x}\,\Theta\left(\sin\omega t\right),\,0,\,0\right),
\end{equation}

where $\omega=2\pi f$, $f$ is the frequency given by \texttt{external potential freq},
and $\Theta$ denotes the step function, equal to 1 for a positive
argument, $1/2$ for a vanishing one and 0 for a negative one; the
phase given by \texttt{external potential phase} is not applied to
the rectangular waves. If the waveform is modeled as produced by an RC circuit,
the rectangular value is replaced by
the exponential increase $E_{x}\left[1-\exp\left(-t'/RC\right)\right]$
during the half-periods in which the field is switched on, and by the
exponential decay $E_{x}\exp\left(-t''/RC\right)$ during those in which
it is switched off, where $t'$ and $t''$ are the times elapsed since the
field was switched on and off, respectively. Here, $RC$ denotes a
decay time (seconds) given by \texttt{external potential time}. The last case describes an orthogonal rotating
electric field. Here the electric field takes the form

\begin{equation}
\vec{E}=\left(E_{x},\,E_{y}\cos\left(\omega t+\varphi\right),\,E_{z}\sin\left(\omega t+\varphi\right)\right),
\end{equation}
where $\left(E_{x},E_{y},E_{z}\right)=\left(\Phi_{x},\Phi_{y},\Phi_{z}\right)/h$,
with the three components $\Phi_{x}$, $\Phi_{y}$, $\Phi_{z}$ given by
\texttt{external potential vector}, and $\varphi$ is the initial phase
given by \texttt{external potential phase}. A time-dependent field
(type other than 0) is not run by the device step of the OpenACC build
and does not use the pre-generated Gaussian pool (Sec \ref{sec:Noise-pool}).

Details on the main effects of orthogonal rotating electric fields
are available in Ref. \cite{lauricella2017effects}.
