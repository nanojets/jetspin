\section{Test Case 25: evaporative counterpart of Test Case 24\label{sec:Test-Case-25}}

{\sloppy
Test Case 25 is Test Case 24 with Yarin evaporation enabled.  The two input
files differ by two directives, \texttt{evaporation yes} and
\texttt{evaporation polymer frac 0.50d0}.  Everything else is shared: the
canonical electrostatic parameters (\texttt{density charge 44000},
\texttt{collector distance 16}, \texttt{external potential 30.02076857}),
Maxwell rheology with $\mu_0=20$ P and $G_0=5\times10^{4}$ dyn/cm$^{2}$,
stochastic Platen air drag, nozzle insertion and collector removal from Test
Case 23, the single-nozzle-bead start and print directives from Example 3,
and Example 5's refinement cadence.  The evaporation law of
Sec.~\ref{sec:Evaporation} is used with the coefficients of
Ref.~\cite{yarin2001bending} unchanged, $B=7$, $m=0.1$, $t_{\mathrm{ev}}=1$,
and a relative humidity of 0.165 at 293.15 K; only the initial polymer mass
fraction is raised from 0.06 to 0.50.\par}

With $c_{p,0}=0.50$ the solidification cutoff $c_p=0.9$ is reached at
$V/V_0=0.556$, and every bead collected in the reference run has reached it:
at the collector the jet is 90\% polymer by mass, 89\% of the initial solvent
has evaporated, the viscosity has grown by a factor of 2.49 (to 49.7 P), the
relaxation time by 1.80, and the elastic modulus by 1.38.

The initial polymer fraction of Ref.~\cite{yarin2001bending} does not survive
this configuration.  With $c_{p,0}=0.06$ the run stops on the code's
numerical instability check at step 1,078,685 on an NVIDIA A30 (step
1,067,695 with the NVFORTRAN CPU build), with a stress NaN.  The cause lies
in the parameter regime.  An evaporating element keeps its charge while its
mass and cross-section decrease with its volume, down to $V/V_0=c_{p,0}/0.9$,
that is one fifteenth; its charge-to-mass ratio therefore grows fifteenfold.
Because $\theta\propto c_p$, the elastic modulus grows only 2.9 times, so the
elastic resistance $G\,\pi a^2$ of a dried element is about five times weaker
than that of the fresh jet.  The dried head of the jet is then pushed
laterally by Coulomb repulsion, its off-axis distance growing from 3 to 20 cm
within 40,000 steps; the next accepted refinement event has to remesh a jet
that has left the physical regime, and the stress NaN follows.  Diagnostic
variants confirm that the outcome follows the elastic resistance of the dried
jet: freezing viscosity, relaxation time and modulus leads to an earlier
failure; keeping the relaxation time constant, so that the modulus grows with
the viscosity, fails when the growth at the cutoff is 10 or less and runs
cleanly when it is 30 or more.  With the relative humidity set to one, so
that no net evaporation occurs, the evaporative code path reproduces Test
Case 24.  Raising the initial polymer fraction bounds the mass loss without
altering the concentration law, whose coefficients $B$ and $m$ were fitted by
Yarin et al.\ to the envelope cone of a much more viscous 6\% PEO solution
($\mu_0=10^{4}$ P, $\theta_0=10$ ms).

The reference run uses the NVFORTRAN 24.3 CPU build and is truncated at
5,000,000 steps, 5\% of the target duration; it closes correctly.  The jet
first reaches the collector at step 2,120,839.  Between 4 and 5 million steps
the collector velocity is 2535 cm/s, the off-axis distance of the leading
bead 2.8 cm, the cone angle 19.7 degrees, the active-bead count between 242
and 295, the path length 112 cm, and the fibre radius at the collector
2.8~$\mu$m against a nozzle radius of 50~$\mu$m.  The envelope cone opens from
the nozzle with a local half-angle growing from about 8 to 12 degrees.  Four
more CPU seeds give mean active counts between 267.8 and 268.4 and the same
path length, so the stationary regime does not depend on the noise
realization.  One NVIDIA A30 runs the same 5 million steps in about 770 s
against 5893 s for the CPU build; it reads the same Gaussian pool, stays on the CPU
trajectory until about 3 million steps, and gives the same stationary
values.
Compared with an NVFORTRAN CPU run of Test Case 24 over the same interval
(3.8 cm, 27 degrees),
evaporation and the stiffening it causes make the bending loops smaller, as
reported in Ref.~\cite{yarin2001bending}.  A full-length reference does not
yet exist.

The A30 run has two phases.  Until the jet holds 100 beads (step 1,446,413
with seed 317, right after an accepted refinement event) the whole step runs
on the host, which also computes the Coulomb sums while the jet has fewer
than 128 active beads, and the run reproduces the CPU build byte for byte.
Afterwards the step runs on the device-resident state, and an ordinary step
returns only a 20-byte record of topology decisions to the host.  With each
process bound to the NUMA node of its GPU, the first phase takes 156 s, the
time of the CPU build; until 2026-10-05 the three Coulomb sums of each step
were offloaded, each call copying 13 arrays to the device and 2 back, and the
phase took 430 s, 79\% of it in those calls.  The second phase takes 612 s,
about 170~$\mu$s per step from 120 to 300 beads against 0.5 to 1.8 ms for
the CPU build: its 31 kernels run on one asynchronous queue and the host
waits once per step, for the topology record.  With a synchronization after
every kernel, as until 2026-10-05, the phase took 1418 s and the device was
idle for 40 to 45\% of each step.  Without
binding, placement of the host process alone changes the timings by up to
8\%.

The case is excluded from the short smoke and regression matrices because of
its length.  The input is stored in \texttt{examples/input-25/input.dat}, and
\texttt{docs/examples/test-25.md} lists the diagnostic variants and the time
budget of the two phases in detail.
